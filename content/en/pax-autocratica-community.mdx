## What each community surface is good for

Announcements and update threads are useful for discovering what changed, but they still need a date and build context. Player discussions are useful for finding symptoms, questions and workarounds worth investigating. They are not a promise that every player has the same issue or that a suggested action works on the current build.

This distinction keeps the community page practical. If a reader wants to ask whether a feature exists, start from an official product statement. If a reader wants to compare a bug symptom or learn whether others saw it, read the discussion, then look for an official confirmation before changing files or making a purchase decision.

## How to use a player post without overtrusting it

A community post becomes more useful when it includes the game build, platform, reproducible symptom and exact source link. A post that only says 'it is broken' can be a lead, but it cannot establish a universal bug. The same applies to recommendations: a configuration that helps one machine or one Early Access build may not apply to another.

When a post points to a download, check whether the link is part of the official public directory or an independently maintained tool. Keep the question and the evidence separate: the community tells you what to investigate, while the official store, site or update record tells you what the game currently promises.

1. Record the post date, build and platform before repeating its claim.
1. Look for more than one independent player report when judging whether a symptom is widespread.
1. Cross-check fixes and feature claims against the official site, store or dated update source.
1. Do not upload passwords, account cookies or private logs into a public discussion.
