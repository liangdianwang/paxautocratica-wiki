# Pax Autocratica Gameplay

Pax Autocratica is a hybrid of totalitarian colony management and an FPS roguelite. The practical loop is to run the colony and state apparatus, assign labor and issue policies, prepare an expedition, lead the frontier battle in first person, then use the people, resources and cores you bring back to change the next decision. This page is a source-backed starting map for the loop; it does not invent keyboard controls or promise a universal build order.

## The gameplay loop: state first, frontier second

The game is not a conventional real-time strategy title where the whole experience stays in an overhead base view. Its identity comes from the handoff between two layers: a colony where the player manages production, citizens, policies and social pressure, and a frontier where the player personally leads troops through first-person combat. The Steam description, official videos and the developer AMA all point to that hybrid as the central reason to play.

That means a battle is not an isolated match. The state you build determines what you can take into an expedition, while the expedition returns with consequences and progression material. Read each outing as a decision about what the colony can afford to risk, what kind of force it can support, and which result will make the next cycle stronger.

## 1. Stabilize the colony before you leave

The opening management job is to make the colony function rather than to chase the most dramatic combat option immediately. The official store page describes citizens being assigned to mining, gathering, hauling, research, manufacturing weapons and armor, and producing food or supplies. Those jobs are the practical foundation for any expedition: without a working flow of people and materials, the frontier layer has nothing reliable to draw on.

Policies are part of the same preparation, not a separate flavor screen. Official material and the developer's explanation describe fear, propaganda, surveillance, punishment, comfort, loyalty and relationships as tools that shape the population. The point is not that one policy is always best; it is that the colony's political condition is itself a resource and a risk.

1. Give citizens jobs that keep basic resources and supplies moving before committing to a long expedition.
1. Check which research or manufacturing direction supports the force you intend to take out, instead of spending every early resource on unrelated upgrades.
1. Treat decrees and social pressure as part of the expedition plan: a policy that raises output can also change fear, loyalty or rebellion risk.

## 2. Turn the colony into an expedition plan

Once the colony is producing and the state apparatus has a direction, the next question is what kind of frontier problem the current force can handle. The game advertises infantry, tanks, mechs, missiles and bullet-hell pressure, while the official videos frame the player as a commander leading squads rather than only aiming a weapon alone. This makes preparation more meaningful than simply selecting the strongest-looking unit: you are matching the force and its resources to the threat you expect to face.

The developer AMA also clarifies an important boundary: the colony and battle are separate layers, and the colony continues while the player is away. A departure is therefore a commitment. Before leaving, finish the management decision that cannot safely wait, make sure the colony can continue without constant attention, and decide what information or reward would justify the time spent in the field.

1. Choose an expedition objective first, then select the troops, vehicles or equipment that answer that objective.
1. Leave enough production and social stability for the colony to keep operating while you are in the battle layer.
1. Do not treat a successful firefight as the only measure of progress; record what the outing returned to the colony and whether it improved the next cycle.

## 3. Read the battle layer as command plus FPS pressure

The frontier side combines direct first-person movement and shooting with the responsibility of commanding troops. The store listing describes infantry, tanks and mechs alongside missiles and bullet-hell encounters, so the useful mental model is not a simple shooter checklist. You are reading space, protecting the force you brought, and deciding when the squad or vehicle layer should carry the pressure while you create an opening.

Exact button prompts and a complete encounter-by-encounter route are not established by the selected sources, so this guide deliberately stops short of pretending to know them. What is supported is the relationship between the layers: the battle is where the state apparatus is tested under pressure, and the result changes what can be built, researched or attempted next.

1. Before an engagement, identify the threat type and the part of your force that is meant to absorb or answer it.
1. During a fight, protect the expedition's ability to return rather than converting every encounter into an all-or-nothing push.
1. After the fight, connect the result back to the colony: note which resource, unit or core changed your next available option.

## 4. Captures and cores are the payoff decisions

Capturing an enemy is more than a victory animation. The Steam page and developer discussion describe bringing captives back and choosing whether to imprison, convert, recruit or sacrifice them. That choice links the frontier to the political and production layer: the same capture can become labor, a new unit, a source of information or an ideological cost, depending on what the colony needs and what kind of state you are building.

The roguelite side adds another reason to inspect the result of an expedition. The store listing names weapon cores, combination cores and troop cores as progression material. Do not read these as a promise that one combination is optimal; read them as the game's way of making the next run a response to what the last run found.

1. Ask what the colony is short of before choosing a use for a capture; the most dramatic option is not automatically the most useful one.
1. Track which core or combination changed your force, so the next expedition is based on a real observed improvement rather than a generic tier list.
1. Keep prisoner, recruitment and sacrifice decisions labelled as political trade-offs; current balance can change the best choice.

## A safe first-session route

For a first session, the safest approach is to learn the handoff between layers before trying to optimize it. This route is intentionally a starting orientation, not a claim that every build or patch uses the same order. It gives a new player a way to ask the right questions while keeping unsupported control details out of the guide.

1. Read the colony as a production system: identify who gathers, hauls, researches, manufactures and supplies before changing several jobs at once.
1. Issue one clear policy direction and observe its effect on the population instead of stacking every available decree immediately.
1. Prepare a modest expedition whose objective and force are understandable; treat the first outing as reconnaissance for the loop.
1. In the frontier, pay attention to the relationship between your first-person actions and the troops or vehicles you command.
1. Return with a record of the reward, capture and core result, then use that evidence to choose the next colony adjustment.
1. Before relying on a control map, damage value or optimal build, re-check the current Steam and community sources for the build you are actually playing.

## What is version-sensitive

Pax Autocratica is still changing, and the Steam Community update trail already records changes to the final-stage objective and default sensitivity. That is why this page treats the loop and system relationships as stable orientation, but avoids hard-coded controls, permanent balance rankings and guaranteed win conditions. Use the official store, official videos and current community hub as the check before turning this overview into a patch-specific route.

## FAQ

### Is Pax Autocratica just an FPS?

No. The first-person frontier battles are connected to a colony and state-management layer where citizens, production and policies shape what you can take into the field.

### What should I do before my first expedition?

Make basic labor and supply production work, choose a clear policy direction, and leave the colony able to continue while you are away. Then take a modest objective and use the result to plan the next cycle.

### Are the controls and best builds covered here?

Not as fixed facts. The selected sources support the gameplay loop and its major systems, but exact controls and optimal patch-specific builds need current hands-on verification.

### What can happen to captured enemies?

The current source set describes prisoner, recruitment/conversion and sacrifice choices. Treat the best use as a context-dependent colony decision, not a universal ranking.
